## Owned-page gap

The live Vietnamese/English local-business website page already says its form is an enquiry, not a booking, and says the offer excludes payments, booking, dispatch, Google management and custom integrations. The product is described as a mobile service website. It did not explicitly answer inventory, cart, online checkout or order management. The thread raised exactly that scope boundary for a gift shop.

Decision: state directly in the canonical COPY/PRICING contracts and the existing bilingual website-service page that Starter is an information/enquiry site, not an online store with inventory or transaction/order management. This is expectation-setting, not a new ecommerce capability or a demand claim. The user may still display owner-approved product/service information; this clarification excludes inventory operations and transactional storefront workflows.

Strongest objection: a longer exclusion list could make the product sound smaller and distract service businesses. The tradeoff is a clearer buyer-fit check and less scope ambiguity. Keep it in the service-scope/FAQ area, not the hero.

Stop/acceptance: continue only if the bilingual page and canonical docs agree. A passing build proves rendering, not lower refund/support rates. Review actual enquiry qualification and scope objections before claiming business benefit.
